An end-to-end mobile product design case study crafted in Figma—transforming the multi-step food ordering journey into an intuitive, warm, and delightful digital experience.

Project Overview

Qyta is an original mobile product design conceptualized from scratch in Figma as a school capstone—built to demonstrate how strong visual hierarchy and empathetic layout reduce decision fatigue during food discovery.

The Challenge

Food delivery apps must present dense information—menus, dietary tags, ratings, delivery ETAs, promotions—on compact screens. The design challenge was organizing this density without creating cognitive overload for hungry users.

The Solution

I designed a cohesive multi-screen system with consistent component states, prominent primary actions, sticky order summary trays, and live prototype micro-interactions that make ordering feel natural and transparent.

Experience Highlights

End-to-End User Journey

Design Retrospective

What I Learned

Designing Qyta reinforced that UI/UX is fundamentally about problem solving rather than visual embellishment. Modeling every screen from the perspective of a user ordering lunch on a busy workday forced me to be ruthless about clarity, touch target sizing, and feedback visibility. Learning Figma's advanced component systems and smart animation flows proved invaluable for bridging the gap between design theory and production implementation.
